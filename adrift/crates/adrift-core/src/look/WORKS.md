# Visual: the works Adrift is measured against

Five real works from the field of drawing the sea, a globe, and one small warm thing on a cool
field, each with what it does that Adrift's look must match. Judged against the running build, never
against descriptions (CRITICS.md).

1. **earth.nullschool.net** (Cameron Beccario, 2013–): the ocean's surface currents as light
   streaks over a dark globe, one hue, no chrome but a date and a menu that hides. What it does
   that we must match: the sea's motion is legible at the whole Earth and at a coast with the same
   marks, and nothing on the screen competes with the water. Our sea has Terra's streamlines and
   running light already; the float must be the only warm thing, and the date and credits must sit
   as quietly as its date does.
2. **Windy.com** (2014–): particle animation of wind and current over a map, read daily by
   sailors. What it does: a trail's density says speed at a glance (particles bunch where the air
   slows, stretch where it runs), and the overlay reads on any ground colour. Our wake's beads must
   bunch in an eddy and spread in a jet so plainly that nobody needs to be told the rule.
3. **Journey** (thatgamecompany, 2012): one red-cloaked figure on a vast cool field (sand, then
   snow), readable at every distance; its scarf and footprints are the only record of where you
   went, and they fade. What it does: the one warm thing is small, never bigger than it must be,
   and the eye finds it across the whole screen; a trail is a memory, not a line. The float, its
   lamp and the comet's tail of beads are held to this.
4. **The Japanese glass fishing float (ukidama)**: a hand-blown sphere of sea-green glass with a
   seal button, netted, which the Pacific carries for years and lands on beaches with its light
   caught inside. What it does: the thing itself is the design. The float is glass (a cool wash
   with a dark rim and one highlight where the sun is, as the sea's sheen is upper-left), its lamp
   amber inside the glass, and nothing on it is a UI pin.
5. **Apple Weather's precipitation map and Apple Maps' globe** (iOS 15–17): a live rendered map
   held to the platform's reading sizes, every mark on a halo, the chrome drawn in the same light
   as the map, at 120 Hz on the device. What it does: the size of every mark is set for the eye at
   the device's distance, not for the pixels, in sun and at night, and no mark flickers between
   states. Our sizes are points at Terra's reading scale (palette.json › sizes), the night palette
   is its own, and a mark is shown fully or not at all.
