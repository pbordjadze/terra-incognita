# The monsoon's sails

A runtime-4 pack: the globe draws dhows as sprites (their frames, furled and under sail, the speck of a
sail from afar and the doorway, drawn from code by `tools/monsoon/art.js` through
`tools/packart/render.cjs`), the wind as streak sprites, the ports as rings, and a year at the foot of
the screen, which is also the globe's season. The script says nothing on the screen but the name; this
file and the panel say what is true and what is drawn.

## What is true

- The monsoon reverses: the south-west monsoon blows from about June to September (its onset over Kerala
  in early June, its withdrawal through October), the north-east monsoon from late October through the
  winter (India Meteorological Department, https://mausam.imd.gov.in/). The sailing seasons of the
  dhow trade followed it: out from Africa and Arabia to India in the spring before the summer wind grew
  strong and again in its late weeks, and home from November on the north-east wind (the Periplus of
  the Erythraean Sea, first century CE, tr. Casson 1989; Villiers, *Sons of Sinbad*, 1940).
- The ports are historical ports of the trade: Kilwa, Zanzibar, Mombasa, Mogadishu, Aden, Muscat, Sur,
  Siraf, Mandvi and Calicut (Kozhikode), placed at their modern towns.

## What is drawn, and not claimed

- The ways between ports are drawn with waypoints to keep them at sea; they are not recorded tracks.
  Which ports are paired is a choice among the many pairings of the trade.
- Each ship's sailing dates are drawn within the seasons, and its day's run of about 160 km is a round
  figure for a dhow with a fair wind. Two ships to a way is for the picture, not a count.
- The wind streaks are drawn where the seasonal winds blow, with the direction of the season and
  lighter in the turning months; they are not from a wind record.
- The year at the foot is the current year; the ships repeat it.
