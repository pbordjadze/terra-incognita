# The vine's long walk · ვაზის გრძელი გზა

A runtime-4 pack: the globe draws the vine's stems as lines (one branch, one layer, the older thicker),
its leaves and clusters as symbols, the tendrils at the growing tips as sprites, and the qvevri at the
origin; the art is drawn from code by `tools/vine/art.js` through `tools/packart/render.cjs`, the ways
and leaves built by `tools/vine/build.py`. Nothing is hand-edited. The script says nothing on the screen
but the name; this file and the panel say what is true and what is drawn.

## What is true

- The earliest evidence of grape wine: jars from Gadachrili Gora and Shulaveris Gora, Kvemo Kartli,
  Georgia, about 6000–5800 BCE (McGovern et al. 2017, PNAS 114: E10309,
  https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1714728114). The origin of the vine here is Gadachrili Gora, at
  41.45° N 44.75° E (the village of Imiri; the site's published coordinates are rounded).
- The spread through the ancient Near East and the Aegean: Hajji Firuz Tepe (c. 5400 BCE), Godin Tepe
  (c. 3500 BCE), the Levant, Egypt (wine jars by 3000 BCE), Crete and Greece (McGovern 2003, *Ancient
  Wine*). The Phoenicians and Greeks in the west, Rome's vineyards up the Rhône, the Mosel and the Rhine,
  the Danube (Unwin 1991, *Wine and the Vine*). The Silk Road: the vine in China by the second century
  BCE (Zhang Qian's embassy to Ferghana, the *Shiji*). The colonial plantings: Hispaniola 1490s, Mexico
  1520s, Peru 1540s, Chile 1550s, Mendoza 1561, the Cape 1655, San Diego 1769, Sydney 1788, Kerikeri
  1819 (Johnson 1989, *Vintage*; Unwin 1991).
- The qvevri: the clay vessel, buried to its mouth, of Georgian winemaking since the Neolithic and still
  (UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage, 2013).

## What is drawn, and not claimed

- The ways are drawn: the vine went by many hands, as cuttings and seeds, often by sea, and the lines
  take the straightest way between the places the record names, with a few turns to keep them at sea or
  on land. No line is the route of any one vine.
- The years are the earliest evidence at or near each place, rounded, and between places interpolated:
  a year on a stem between two places means nothing exact. Katsunuma's 718 is a legend, kept as one.
- Which places are named is a choice; many are left out (Mesopotamia, the Black Sea's other coasts,
  India, the Rhine's many valleys). The leaves' and clusters' places along the stems are drawn.
- The seasons are the reader's own, from the device's month, not any place's.
