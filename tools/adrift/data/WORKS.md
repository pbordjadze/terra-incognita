# Truth: the works Adrift is measured against

The craft is the honesty of the thing: real data, told apart from what is drawn from it, so that
nothing on the screen claims more than its source can bear. These are the works that do it best,
and what each does that Adrift must match.

1. **Plastic Adrift** (Erik van Sebille, with Erik Behrens; adrift.org.au, 2012–). Tap the sea and
   watch where a rubber duck would go over ten years, as a cloud of probability from the real
   drifters' transition matrix (van Sebille, England and Froyland 2012). What it does that Adrift
   must match: the maybes are honest. It never shows one path as *the* path; the cloud is the
   drifters' own statistics; the page says it is a typical year and a rubber duck, not a forecast.
   Adrift's maybes (the pale specks) must spread as the measured diffusivity says, and the one
   real path (k = 0) must never be dressed up as more than one draw from that cloud.
2. **earth.nullschool.net** (Cameron Beccario, 2013–). Every pixel is a model's field at a named
   hour, with its source and the time stamped on the screen; nothing is painted in; the date you
   see is the date of the data. What Adrift must match: the reading of the date. Our sea is a
   climatology, so the screen must say "a typical year", never a given day; the day of the year
   moves the month, and nothing on the screen claims it is this year's sea.
3. **OpenDrift** (Knut-Frode Dagestad et al., MET Norway, 2018–), and the leeway taxonomy it
   carries (Allen and Plourde 1999; Breivik et al. 2011). Search-and-rescue drift from real
   object classes, each with a measured leeway coefficient and its error, run as an ensemble so the
   uncertainty is in the picture. What Adrift must match: a coefficient with a source and an error
   bar, and an ensemble whose spread is the measured spread, not a decorative one.
4. **The Global Drifter Program's own drifter map** (NOAA AOML; the GDP's interactive map of
   buoys and tracks). Each buoy has an identifier, a deployment, a death; a track is a track of a
   real buoy, with the days it was alive. What Adrift must match: the silver drifter that rises
   among the maybes is a named buoy, a real year, and its day on the screen is not the float's day.
5. **Terra's own `tools/ocean/SOURCES.md`.** The measured/drawn table, the credit owed, the
   plain statement of what is a guess (the smoothed-in currents where no drifter went). What Adrift
   must match: the same table for every sheet, and a B channel of "how measured" wherever the
   rules might otherwise ride a guess without saying so.

Judged against these, Adrift must: name its date honestly; make the maybes the data's spread;
carry a sourced leeway with its error; keep real drifters real; and say, in the app, what is
measured and what is drawn.
