# Ink orders (spike)

**The idea.** Your strokes are orders, and the ink is alive. Each stroke is a channel with a direction (the way your finger went), and any Carthaginian unit that steps within its reach is carried along it, the way a Tsuro tile carries a piece. Pulses of ink run down every stroke as it carries men. Where a stroke ends the ink pools, and that means "hold here, then find the fight". A body of men that the ink has partly caught goes to the channel too, so one line drawn through a block peels the whole block off after it.

**What's novel.** There's no unit selection, no arrows and no UI. Three or four brush strokes on paper are the whole plan, and roughly 3,000 tick marks (each a man's shoulders, or a horse a longer stroke) decide the rest by local rules. Every fallen unit leaves a faint stain on the paper for good, so the battle's shape stays on the page afterwards as its record.

**The rules (sim.js).** Each unit follows the ink if it's in reach, or else heads for the nearest enemy within a short radius (much further for men the ink has carried and then let go). Bodies take up room: overlaps are pushed apart, so the deep Roman column really shoves. At contact, attrition is Lanchester-like: blows land on the nearest foe, harder from the flank and rear and far harder on men who are fleeing. Morale drains from enemies behind you, enemies on both sides, the blows you take, being outnumbered close by, and friends running. Below zero, a unit flees for good. The Romans follow the consuls' intent: advance and press toward the centre. Nothing is scripted. With the good plan (the Africans hooking in, Hasdrubal riding up the river and round the rear), the Roman mass ends as a hollow ring inside a Carthaginian ring and is destroyed. With the bad plan (everything aimed at the centre), the Roman depth breaks the crescent. That's the same rules on the same ground: the probe ends with Rome at 225 formed (930 fallen) and Carthage at 275 formed for the good plan, and Rome at 1032 formed and Carthage at 265 for the bad one.

**What's real underneath.** The ground is Terrarium elevation around Canne della Battaglia, contoured every 3 m, with hachures on the slope up to Cannae's ridge. The Aufidus is traced as the cheapest path along the valley floor (the tiles can't resolve its channel). The deployment follows the sources: maniples in three lines, depth over breadth, about 35 men a unit, so 55k legionaries; the Roman horse by the river, the allied horse on the open flank; Hannibal's convex crescent, the Africans set back on both wings, Hasdrubal's horse by the river, the Numidians on the right. The real day is a faint dashed graphite underdrawing: Hasdrubal's ride, the Africans' turn, the crescent standing and then bent back, the Roman press.

**What's faked.** The frame is turned so the river runs down the left. All the numbers (speeds, attack, morale) were tuned by eye with `probe.cjs` in about an hour. The outcome depends on exactly where a stroke lies: the browser run and the Node probe differ by a few units in stroke sampling, and their final counts differ by around 150 units. It's still always a rout, but less of one in the browser.

**What the finger does.** Drawing a stroke starts the day. Draw from a body of troops outward, and the stroke's direction is the order. Strokes may cross, and where they do the newer ink lies on top.
