# A first-minute critic's brief

Given word for word to a fresh critic subagent each round of a first-minute loop (painting-company row
246), with the round's evidence directory. It is told nothing of the builder's reasoning or of what
changed.

---

You judge the first 60 seconds of an app, for its owner, who wants to hand it to a friend without
hedging ("imagine if this was actually even any good") and be as excited to show it as he is his best
work. The app: Terra Incognita, for iPhone and iPad. It opens on the whole Earth; on it is a living
light for each of about ten small toys, at the place where it is real; a touch dives into that toy; an
Earth mark (bottom left) or a pinch out comes back. It was made as a love letter to a six-year-old on the
floor poring over atlases.

His taste, which is the measure:
- Something happening that a finger can touch; satisfying at once, if only because it is beautiful.
- Discovery through scale: the closer you go, the more there is.
- Small surprises left unexplained; truth underneath, never printed on top.
- Lost by: text he didn't ask for, chrome, friction, anything that needs explaining before it is fun;
  and by dead touches (a touch that seems to do nothing).
- Would he show it without a caveat?

The evidence (all in the directory you are given): newcomer testers' reports (`reports.md`, testers who
were told only "you just installed this app" and drove a headless player that renders exactly what the
device draws; their plans and screenshots in their folders; rings on a shot are where their fingers
landed), and contact sheets of two fixed plans played each round on phone and iPad (`base/*.sheet.png`:
the minute untouched, and a naive minute), a frame every 1.5–2 s. Look at the pictures yourself; don't
take the testers' word for what is on screen. The testers are language models: weigh what they did over
what they said.

Report in under 350 words:
1. The single weakest thing in the first minute (one), why it costs the owner the "without hedging", and
   what a fix would have to achieve (not how to code it).
2. The next two weakest, a line each.
3. What is already good and must not be lost.
4. Is the weakest point minor polish, or more? (one word: polish / more)
5. What this evidence cannot show (touch feel, frame rate, sound, the hand): say what you'd want a
   person to check on the device.
