# When the sea was low

A runtime-4 pack: the globe draws eight pictures of the continental shelves as dry land (built by
`tools/lowsea/build_shelves.py` from Natural Earth), the old rivers and a lake as geography, and
mammoths as sprites (drawn from code by `tools/lowsea/art.js` through `tools/packart/render.cjs`).
Nothing is hand-edited. The script says nothing on the screen but the name; this file and the panel say
what is true and what is drawn.

## What is true

- At the Last Glacial Maximum, about 20,000 years ago, the sea stood about 120–130 m below today's
  (Lambeck et al. 2014, PNAS 111: 15296, https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1411762111). The shelves drawn
  were land then: Doggerland (Gaffney, Thomson and Fitch 2007), Sundaland (Voris 2000, J. Biogeogr. 27:
  1153), Sahul, Beringia, the Gulf, the northern Adriatic and the Black Sea's shelf, Patagonia's.
- The rivers are drawn in outline after the sea floor's valleys: the Channel River (Gupta et al. 2007,
  Nature 448: 342), the Elbe's course across the North Sea, the North Sunda (Molengraaff) river (Voris
  2000), the Tigris–Euphrates down the Gulf (Lambeck 1996). Lake Carpentaria (Reeves et al. 2008).
- Mammoth bones are dredged from the North Sea floor and found across Beringia.

## What is drawn, and not claimed

- The land that comes up is the floor shallower than 200 m (Natural Earth's K_200 bathymetry, public
  domain, `LICENSE-NATURAL-EARTH.md`), not the 120 m contour: the ice age's shore lay a little inside
  what is drawn, and the drawing is at Natural Earth's generalisation.
- The rivers' and lake's lines are generalised sketches, not mapped channels; the mammoths' number and
  ways are drawn. The sea going out in seconds stands for thousands of years, and nothing is said of
  the ice sheets themselves, which are not drawn.
