# Whales in the desert

A runtime-4 pack: the globe draws four pictures of a sea over Egypt's Western Desert, faded in from the
deep and out from the shore (`tethys-1.png` to `-4.png`, built by `tools/whales/build_sea.py` from
Natural Earth land), sprites for the whales (their frames, blow, dive, stranding and bones drawn from
code by `tools/whales/art.js` through `tools/packart/render.cjs`), and the bones as symbols. Nothing is
hand-edited. The script says nothing on the screen but the name; this file and the panel say what is
true and what is drawn.

## What is true

- Wadi Al-Hitan (Whale Valley), Egypt, 29°20′ N 30°11′ E: hundreds of skeletons of archaeocete whales
  from the late Eocene, about 37 million years ago, when the area was a shallow bay of the Tethys Sea.
  UNESCO World Heritage List, inscribed 2005 (https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1186/). The pack's doorway
  and first bones sit at UNESCO's reference point for the property, not at an excavation.
- *Basilosaurus isis*: a long, slender whale, to about 18 m, with small hind limbs (Gingerich, Smith
  and Simons 1990, *Science* 249: 154–157). *Dorudon atrox*: about 5 m (Uhen 2004, Papers on
  Paleontology 34, University of Michigan). Both are drawn from above after the skeletons' proportions.
- The sea withdrew over millions of years as the Tethys closed; the whales did not strand.

## What is drawn, and not claimed

- The shore of the drawn sea is a generalised illustration of a late Eocene coast across northern Egypt
  and Sinai (the Tethys reaching the Fayum), not a reconstruction at any date. It is drawn only over
  today's land (Natural Earth 1:10m land, public domain; `LICENSE-NATURAL-EARTH.md`), so today's seas are
  the globe's own.
- The whales' colour, skin and ways through the water are invented. The three further bone sites near
  the valley are illustrative: Eocene whale bones are found across the Fayum's beds, but these exact
  places are not claimed. Where a whale strands is where it happened to be when the water went.
- The sea's coming and going in seconds stands for millions of years.
