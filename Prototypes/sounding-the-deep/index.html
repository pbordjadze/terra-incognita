Sounding the deep

HMS Challenger, station by station

HMS Challenger was a British ship sent out from 1872 to 1876 to explore the deep ocean. At its stations the crew stopped, let a weighted line down to measure the depth, and brought up samples of the sea floor.

Reading the globe

  • Dots are the stations in this record: filled once the date has reached them, hollow still ahead.
  • The dashed line between two close stations is an illustration of the passage, not the ship's logged course; it is drawn only when they are two weeks apart or less and the straight way between them stays off land.
  • Across a longer gap the record has no position, so the ship is not shown.
  • The hanging line goes down to the depth recorded at that station, at 26 points to a kilometre on this screen.
  • Today's borders and names are hidden; the coasts are today's, as a reference.

What this record is

94 dated stations, from 2 February 1873 to 6 May 1876, with their positions and recorded depths: the stations whose seabed deposits were examined for manganese by Murray and Renard. It is a selection, not the expedition's complete log, and its positions are nineteenth-century measurements, not modern ones.
