# The vine's long walk · ვაზის გრძელი გზა

A runtime-6 pack built on the globe's dated geography: every stem, voyage, leaf and cluster carries a
year, is set once, and is drawn by the globe as far as the date has come. Over land the vine is a stem
that thickens with age (`thickens`); over the sea a fine dashed voyage line; the tendrils at the growing
tips are sprites on dated ways. The art is drawn from code by `tools/vine/art.js` through
`tools/packart/render.cjs`; the ways, twigs, leaves and clusters are built by `tools/vine/build.py` over
the globe's own land. Nothing is hand-edited. The script writes nothing on the screen but the name and,
when something is touched, a place and the year the vine arrived there; this file and the panel say what
is true and what is drawn.

## What is true

- **The earliest wine.** Jars from Gadachrili Gora and Shulaveris Gora, Kvemo Kartli, Georgia, hold
  tartaric acid and the other markers of grape wine, about 6000–5800 BCE (McGovern et al. 2017, PNAS
  114: E10309, https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1714728114). The vine's origin here is Gadachrili Gora, at
  41.45° N 44.75° E (the published coordinates are rounded). The qvevri, the clay vessel buried to its
  mouth, is the vessel of Georgian winemaking since the Neolithic and still (UNESCO Intangible Cultural
  Heritage, 2013). The Areni-1 cave in Armenia has the oldest known winery (about 4100 BCE); Hajji Firuz
  Tepe in the Zagros has wine jars of about 5400 BCE, Godin Tepe of about 3500 BCE (McGovern 2003).
- **The ancient spread.** The Levant and Egypt (wine jars at Abydos by about 3000 BCE); Cyprus, Crete
  and Greece in the Bronze Age; the Phoenicians to Carthage (traditionally 814 BCE), Ibiza, Cádiz and
  Iberia; the Greeks to Sicily (730s BCE), southern Italy and Massalia (about 600 BCE), and round the
  Black Sea (Olbia about 600 BCE, Chersonesus 422 BCE); the Etruscans and Rome up Italy, the Rhône, to
  Bordeaux, the Mosel and the Rhine, the Danube and Pannonia; Thrace's wine in Homer (Unwin 1991,
  McGovern 2003). The vine in China: Zhang Qian's embassy to Ferghana, second century BCE (the *Shiji*);
  Turpan's grapes; on through Korea to Japan, where Katsunuma's 718 is a legend, kept as one.
- **The oceans.** Hispaniola 1490s, Veracruz and Mexico 1520s, Parras (the Americas' oldest winery,
  1597), New Mexico 1629 and El Paso 1650s, Peru 1540s, Chile 1548–1555, Mendoza 1561, São Vicente 1532,
  the Cape 1655 (the first wine pressed 1659), Stellenbosch 1679, Constantia 1685, San Diego 1769 and the
  missions north to Sonoma 1823, the First Fleet at Sydney 1788, Tasmania 1823, the Hunter 1828, the
  Barossa 1842, Kerikeri 1819, Hawke's Bay 1851, Central Otago 1864, Marlborough 1973, the Willamette
  1965, Margaret River 1967, Yantai 1892, Ningxia 1984 (Johnson 1989; Unwin 1991; Robinson & Harding
  2015, *The Oxford Companion to Wine*).

## What is drawn, and not claimed

- The ways are drawn: the vine went by many hands, as cuttings and seeds, often by sea, and the lines
  follow the coasts, valleys and roads the record names, swaying a little as a vine does. No line is the
  route of any one vine. Where the globe's own coast says a way is over sea it is a voyage line, over land
  a stem: a coast grazed for fewer than three points takes its neighbours' kind, so stems do not flicker
  along a shore.
- The years are the earliest evidence at or near each place, rounded; between places they are
  interpolated, so a year read along a stem between two places means nothing exact. A twig to a wine
  region grows out in the years before the region's date, from the nearest trunk the vine had reached by
  then; the trunk it leaves from is a choice of nearness, not a record.
- Which places are named is a choice; many are left out. Eastern North America is left out on purpose:
  the European vine planted at Jamestown in 1619 and after failed, and the wine made there until the
  twentieth century was of native grapes and hybrids, which are not this vine. India is drawn only as far
  as the Persian and Greek introductions (Kapisa, Taxila); its modern wine regions are not.
- The leaves' places along the stems are drawn; their seasons are the reader's own month, each
  hemisphere its own, not any year's weather. The clusters are green from April to August and ripe
  otherwise in the north, the reverse in the south.
- The stem's thickness is age, nothing measured: a stem is thin where the vine has just arrived and
  thickens over 2,500 years.
