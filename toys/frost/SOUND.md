# Frost: what it should sound like (row 254, written before reading the tools)

The owner: "Frost is very good just a little more musicality now." Today the sound is a crackle you
hear, a rate of clicks following the freezing. It should be something you play with a finger.

- **The pane is a glass instrument.** At −50 °C a window is a hard, ringing plate. A touch on the glass
  is struck glass: a soft, glassy tone, its pitch by where you touch (higher up the pane, higher; one
  pentatonic scale, so any two touches sound together). Bare glass rings longer and clearer; thick frost
  damps the strike to a short, muffled tick.
- **The warm finger on ice** sounds like ice giving way: a soft wet sizzle while it melts, louder over
  thick frost and silent over bare glass, and small dry snaps as a drag breaks through ferns, more when
  it moves faster. You hear the stroke as you make it.
- **The regrowth answers.** As the wet glass freezes again behind the finger, the new crystals ring:
  tiny high glassy pings, on the same scale, in the order the stroke was drawn, a beat after it, sparse
  and fading as the fringe fills. A gesture is a phrase; the window plays it back to you, quieter, a few
  seconds later. This is the music: call and answer.
- **The frame's own growth** stays the quiet bed it is: a fine, sparse, dry crackle that thins as the
  glass fills, so the playing is heard over it.
- **A shake**: a soft wet rush across the glass, then the creep's crackle returning.
- Cold and sparse: silence between, no drone, no clicks or pops, nothing loud. Phones held close: soft
  levels, a gentle top end.
