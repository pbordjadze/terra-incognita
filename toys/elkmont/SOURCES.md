# Elkmont: sources

What is measured, what is drawn, and from where. No data file is bundled: the woods are drawn from code
(src/forest.rs) and the fireflies follow the rules below (src/swarm.rs).

## The place and the night (measured)

- **Elkmont, Great Smoky Mountains National Park, Tennessee** (35.656° N, 83.582° W), by the Little River,
  where *Photinus carolinus* gathers in numbers each June and flashes in step; the park runs its viewing
  nights there.

## The fireflies (measured rules, drawn numbers)

- **The males' burst**: four to eight bright flashes over two to four seconds, then a dark spell of about
  eight to twelve seconds; the swarm's bursts come about every twelve seconds (*Photinus carolinus*,
  Wikipedia, after L. Faust, "Natural history and flash repertoire of the synchronous firefly *Photinus
  carolinus*", Florida Entomologist 93 (2010) 208–217).
- **Why the beat**: R. Sarfati, K. Joshi, O. Martin, J. C. Hayes, S. Iyer-Biswas, O. Peleg, "Emergent
  periodicity in the collective synchronous flashing of fireflies", *eLife* 12 (2023) e78908. Alone, a male
  has no rhythm: he waits a random, broadly spread while between bursts. Together, a flash sets off any
  male who is rested, so the first to start starts them all, and the swarm's beat is the shortest wait
  among many. Here: after his burst a male rests until the woods round him have been dark a while
  (8.2 s, a little different for each), then waits his own random while (exponential, mean 38 s) or
  starts at once on seeing any flash.
- **The females' answer**: a female answers from the ground, in the males' dark spell, with a doublet (two
  flashes close together), about three seconds after the males' last flash; males that see an answer fly
  to her (Faust 2010; A. Moiseff and J. Copeland, "Firefly synchrony: a behavioral strategy to minimize
  visual clutter", *Science* 329 (2010) 181, whose females answered LEDs flashing as males do).
- **The colour**: yellow-green, near 560 nm.

## Drawn (not measured)

- Every number the sources don't give: how many fly (1,100 males, 90 females), how far a flash is seen
  (30 m), a male's answering delay (0.22–0.32 s) and how far he is pulled into step within a burst, a
  burst ending once he sees no others flashing, how they fly (0.55 m/s, wandering), how they come out
  over the first seventy seconds, the light's size and glow.
- **The finger's light**: an LED the fireflies read as a male: held, it gives one burst of six flashes,
  and the nearest females may answer it; a rested male who sees it starts his burst.
- **The woods**: the trail, trunks, canopy, rhododendron and leaf litter are made up after the place, as is
  the night's haze, the stars in the canopy's gaps, and the dusk at the start.
- **The sound**: the Little River and a far gray treefrog's trill, drawn from noise; the fireflies are
  silent.
