# Screw — what it should be

Written before looking at any tool (painting-company row 250). Build toward it; a tool that falls
short is the tool's bug.

**The feel.** A record turning on a dark page. One tap and a Paul Wall song plays. Put a finger down
and push it toward the floor: the record slows and the voice drops with it, thick and syrupy, the way
a turntable does when you ride its pitch. That is the screw. It stays where he leaves it. Tap and the
record says the last beat again, in time, like it was always meant to: that is the chop. Hold and it
keeps saying it. Flick backward and the record spins back with that wheel-up whine and drops a couple
of bars earlier. Nothing to learn first: no numbers, no BPM, no knobs, no grid.

**What it quietly teaches (the real moves).** Slow and pitch together, never time-stretched: a
turntable's pitch fader, ridden down. Screw tapes sit around two-thirds to three-quarters speed. The
chop is two copies of the same record a beat apart, and cutting between them: the phrase repeats and
the song never loses time. Our tap jumps back exactly one beat of the song, so it lands on the groove
at whatever speed he's at. The rewind ("bring it back") and the brake (the platter stopping under a
hand).

**Oh huh neat, in seconds.** The record's spin is the speed: he sees it slow as he hears it. Each chop
is a little visible kick back in the grooves. The song's shape is a faint ring around the record with
the needle's place on it, so a rewind visibly travels.

**Chrome.** Only the toy conventions: the kit's "i" (its demo: a hand on a slowing record) and "?"
(just the rules, three or four lines). A small sleeve to open a song from Files. The song's name,
quiet. Nothing pinned that isn't the record.

**The song stays out of everything public.** It is his own file, opened from Files, never bundled,
committed, uploaded by CI or fetched.
