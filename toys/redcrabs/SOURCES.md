# Red Crabs: sources

What the toy's rules stand on (Gecarcoidea natalis, the Christmas Island red crab). Not fetched in this
session; to be checked against the pages before the toy's text is final.

- Parks Australia, Christmas Island National Park, "Red crab migration": the migration starts with the
  first heavy rains of the wet season (October to December); tens of millions of adults walk from the
  rainforest to the coast; females release their eggs into the sea at the turn of the high tide before
  dawn in the moon's last quarter; the crabs walk mostly in the early morning and late afternoon and
  shelter from the sun, since they lose water easily; barrier fences along roads lead them to underpasses
  and a crab bridge.
- Adamczewska, A. M. and Morris, S. (2001), "Ecology and behavior of Gecarcoidea natalis, the Christmas
  Island red crab, during the annual breeding migration", Biological Bulletin 200: 305–320: the
  migration's timing by rain, walking speeds and the hours of activity.

What is made up: the island's layout on the screen (one road, one underpass, the shore), the crabs'
number on it (about 2,600), the day's length (50 seconds) and how quickly the forest dries.
