# Swell: rounds

One line a round: what changed, and the clock of each loop step (ET).

- Round 1 (Nov 3, 2026): a tap drops a wave into the real sea; the long-wave equation on GEBCO's depths carries it at sqrt(g h), bending over ridges and shelves, and coasts chime where it arrives; a held finger beats rings; idle, the sea rises where the great tsunamis began (Tōhoku first). Plans `start`, `hold`.
- Round 2 (Nov 3, 2026, the critic's: the sea never settled into calm, the chimes blurred into a bed, the waves were pale and small): the grid's own short ripples taken out (a Shapiro filter every 16 steps), the drop 260 km wide, the coasts take more of each wave, so each tap is one clean crest and the sea calms; chimes at most 1.5 a second, each coast once per arrival (an echo only if taller), shorter; the Pacific fills the screen (zoom 1.3), the crests' light and troughs stronger; 3,600 s of sea a second (a lighter load on the phone).
- Round 3 (Nov 3, 2026, the critic's: where waves reached coasts they smeared into blocky white and dark blotches over the land): the light masked by the globe's own fine coast (nothing on land), the slopes' light giving way within ~40 km of the shore to surf, a line of light as bright as the arriving wave is tall; the sea's height carried into the shore cells so the grid's steps don't show; the Pacific larger (zoom 1.6); Tōhoku at 0.3 s; a held finger's beat a little uneven.
- Round 4 (Nov 3, 2026, the critic's: a pale smudge at first sight, a blown-out white held-finger ring, streaks on the Bering ice and shelf, thin sine chimes): darker troughs and clamped highlights (a crisp front, not a glow); the month's sea ice (September's) taken as shore; the sea floor's friction in the shallows; the drop's note by the depth under the finger (deep low, shelf high) and a breath of water under each chime; zoom 1.45.
