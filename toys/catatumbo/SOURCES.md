# Catatumbo: sources

What is real and what is drawn. The sources were found by web search on Nov 13, 2026 and are cited as
those results gave them. Catatumbo loads no data: the lake, the storm and its sound are made by code.

## Real

- **The place and its storm.** Lake Maracaibo, Venezuela, where the Catatumbo river meets it, is the
  densest lightning on Earth: 232.52 flashes per km² a year (Albrecht et al., *Bulletin of the American
  Meteorological Society*, 2016, as reported at
  https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/researchers-announce-worlds-new-lightning-hot-spot-180958994/),
  its storms coming on about 297 nights a year (the same team's figure, as quoted in the results; older
  counts are lower, see https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catatumbo_lightning).
- **How the channel grows.** Niemeyer, L., Pietronero, L. & Wiesmann, H. J. (1984). Fractal dimension of
  dielectric breakdown. *Physical Review Letters* 52(12), 1033–1036. doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.52.1033. The
  "simplest nontrivial stochastic model for dielectric breakdown": the potential between the electrodes
  obeys Laplace's equation, and a cell beside the discharge breaks down with a chance of the local field
  to a power η (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dielectric_breakdown_model). `bolt.rs` is that model, η = 1.9.
- **The thunder.** Sound in air at about 343 m/s; each piece of the channel heard after its own distance,
  the near pieces first, so a stroke's thunder rolls for as long as its channel is deep.
- **A held finger strikes again.** A flash is several return strokes down the same channel.

## Drawn

The shore and its stilt-house lamps, the cloud, the colours, the eye's 60 m height over the water and
the lens. The leader is slowed (a real one takes hundredths of a second) so it can be seen. The air's
damping of the thunder's highs is a filter by distance, not a measured curve.
